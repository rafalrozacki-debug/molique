Cards

Four card types for different purposes plus a full set of hover effects. Ready-made layouts are in the examples; data tables are covered by a separate page, Tables & Lists.

1. Which card

All four are plain boxes with a border and shadow — what differs is their purpose and what each one adds on top.

Class For what What it adds
.card A universal container: a dashboard panel, a form, a modal's content. Nothing — a plain surface.
.featured-box Presenting a feature or a service on a landing page. A thick top border and a slot for an icon.
.thumb-info Portfolio, gallery, category tiles. A background photo and a title overlay that appears on hover.
.testimonial A customer review. A layout for a quote, a rating, and an author with an avatar.
Header

Card content. Header and footer are optional.

Footer

Featured box

A thick top border in the accent color.

2. The classic card

Three zones, all optional. A plain .card is already complete — add a header and footer only when you have something to put in them. 

<div class="card">
  <div class="card-header">Title</div>
  <div class="card-body">Content…</div>
  <div class="card-footer">Actions</div>
</div>
Class What it does
.card A --bg-surface surface, a border, rounded corners, and a shadow. Clips its content, so a full-width image automatically gets rounded corners too.
.card-header A title bar with a separating line.
.card-body The main padded content. Without it, content sticks to the edges — see pitfall #1.
.card-footer A footer for buttons and metadata; a dimmed background.
A card as a modal's content

Inside <dialog class="modal-dialog">, it's .card that gives the window its header, body, and button bar. There are no separate modal classes for these zones — see Modals, Tabs & Accordions.

3. Featured box and image cards

Featured box

A card for a single product feature. The icon and the thick top border make it a rhythmically repeatable element — three or four side by side in a grid. 

<div class="featured-box">
  <div class="featured-box-icon"></div>
  <h4>Speed</h4>
  <p>Feature description…</p>
</div>

Image cards

A photo with an overlay that slides in on hover. Requires two levels: .thumb-info wraps the whole thing and clips the photo, while .thumb-info-wrapper inside holds the image itself. Without that second level, there's nothing to scale on hover. 

<a href="#" class="thumb-info">
  <span class="thumb-info-wrapper">
    <img src="photo.jpg" alt="">
  </span>
  <span class="thumb-info-title">Project name</span>
</a>
Class Component What it does
.featured-box Featured box A card with an accent border on top.
.featured-box-icon Featured box A round slot for an icon or emoji.
.thumb-info Image card The wrapper; clips the photo and holds the overlay.
.thumb-info-wrapper Image card The layer with the photo — this is what scales on hover.
.thumb-info-title Image card The caption on the overlay.
.thumb-info-bottom Image card A variant: caption at the bottom edge.
.thumb-info-center Image card A variant: caption centered.
.thumb-info-light Image card A lighter overlay — for dark photos.

4. Testimonials

A customer review: a quote, a rating, and an author. The rating is .testimonial-stars, the same mechanism as .star-rating in the shop — you pass the value via the --rating variable, not a class. 

<div class="testimonial">
  <div class="testimonial-stars" style="--rating: 5;"></div>
  <p class="testimonial-quote">The rollout went smoother than we expected.</p>
  <div class="testimonial-author">
    <img class="testimonial-avatar" src="avatar.jpg" alt="">
    <div>
      <div class="testimonial-name">Anna Kowalska</div>
      <div class="testimonial-role">COO</div>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>
Class What it does
.testimonial The review's wrapper.
.testimonial-stars The rating. Driven by the --rating variable (0–5, fractions partially fill a star).
.testimonial-quote The quote's content.
.testimonial-author The footer: an avatar plus the person's info.
.testimonial-avatar The author's photo, cropped to a circle.
.testimonial-name The full name.
.testimonial-role The job title or company.
Several reviews side by side

Drop testimonials into a .carousel and you get a swipeable slider with no extra code. Details in Carousels & Lightbox.

5. Effects and overlapping onto a hero

Hover

The effects only touch transform and opacity, so the GPU handles them and they don't trigger a page layout recalculation. They can be combined on one element.

Class Effect Needs JS
.hover-spring A springy bounce on hover. no
.hover-gpu-shadow A growing shadow without animating box-shadow. no
.hover-scale Enlargement. Strength set by --hover-scale. no
.hover-shadow Lift with a shadow. Height: --hover-y. no
.hover-tilt A fixed 3D tilt on hover. no
.tilt-card A tilt that follows the cursor. Disables itself on touch — saves battery. yes

Overlapping the section above

A classic landing-page layout: a card overlapping the bottom edge of a hero. That's a negative top margin — .overlap-container gives a default value, and its variants let you match it to the card's height.

Class What it does
.overlap-container Pulls the element up. On mobile the offset is smaller, so the card doesn't cover the hero's heading.
.overlap-up-50 Pulls up by about 50px.
.overlap-up-100 Pulls up by about 100px.
.overlap-up-150 Pulls up by about 150px — for tall cards.

6. Pitfalls

1 Content dropped straight into .card

A plain .card has no padding — that's deliberate, so a full-width image or table can reach all the way to the edges. Text dropped in directly, though, sticks right to the border. Wrap it in .card-body or add a spacing class to the card, e.g. .p-4. 

<!-- WRONG - text right against the border -->
<div class="card">Content</div>

<!-- RIGHT -->
<div class="card"><div class="card-body">Content</div></div>
<div class="card p-4">Content</div>
2 An image card without .thumb-info-wrapper

An image dropped straight into .thumb-info displays correctly, so the mistake is easy to miss — nothing simply happens on hover. It's the .thumb-info-wrapper layer that scales, not the <img> itself; without it there's nothing to animate.

3 A rating concatenated into a class name

It's tempting to generate class="stars-4" in your template. That name doesn't exist as a literal in the source, so neither PurgeCSS nor your editor's search will find it. The rating rides in a variable precisely so a value from the backend doesn't create new classes. 

<!-- WRONG -->
<div class="testimonial-stars stars-<?= $rating ?>">

<!-- RIGHT -->
<div class="testimonial-stars" style="--rating: <?= $rating ?>;">
4 A card overlapping a hero hides behind it

.overlap-container lifts the element with a negative margin and gives it a z-index, but if the hero section has its own stacking context (e.g. via position and a higher z-index), the card will end up behind it. Stick to the scale from the --z-index-* variables instead of writing your own numbers.
